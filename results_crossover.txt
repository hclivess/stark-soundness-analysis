1. THEOREM 7(a): where JBR's per-query yield overtakes UDR's
====================================================================================================
   m_eq(R) = sqrt(rho)/(1-sqrt(rho))^2 = u/(u-1)^2,  u = 2^{R/2}

    blowup   R    y_UDR  m_eq closed  m_eq search   ok
  ------------------------------------------------------
         2   1   0.4150       8.2426       8.2426 PASS
         4   2   0.6781       2.0000       2.0000 PASS
         8   3   0.8301       0.8460       0.8460 PASS
        16   4   0.9125       0.4444       0.4444 PASS
        32   5   0.9556       0.2608       0.2608 PASS
        64   6   0.9776       0.1633       0.1633 PASS

  At blowup 2 you need m > 8.24 before the Johnson regime even has a
  better per-query yield than unique decoding. At blowup 8 or more, any
  m >= 1 suffices. Low-blowup systems get much less from JBR than the
  'Johnson beats unique decoding' folklore suggests.

           field   R   g  s* closed   s* scan    ok

        31-bit^4   3  16       95.1      94.5  PASS
    Goldilocks^2   1   0      217.9     218.0  PASS
    Goldilocks^2   1  16      179.4     179.5  PASS
    Goldilocks^2   2   0      143.0     143.0  PASS
    Goldilocks^2   2  16      119.4     119.5  PASS
    Goldilocks^2   3   0      119.2     118.5  PASS
    Goldilocks^2   3  16       99.9      99.0  PASS

  Below s*, use the Johnson regime. Above it, unique decoding is
  strictly better AND rests on a weaker assumption -- UDR needs no
  list-decoding argument at all. Buying queries past s* buys you both
  more security and a cleaner proof.

4. VERIFIED SYSTEMS: which regime should each be reported in?
====================================================================================================
  system                  E  R    s   g     UDR     JBR   m*   best      s*
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  RISC Zero             124  2   50   0    33.9    50.0 971.0687985200308    JBR     137
  Plonky2 std recur     128  3   28  16    39.2    57.9 394.91169934883624    JBR     100
  Miden RECURSIVE_96    128  3   27  16    38.4    56.5 639.3666804821854    JBR     102

  All three deployed systems sit BELOW their crossover, so JBR is the
  right regime to report them in -- and all three land in the 50-70 bit
  band that soundcalc independently reports for Pico (53) and
  Airbender (67). Reaching 100 provable bits means crossing s*, which
  is 130-190 queries: roughly 3-4x what these systems currently use.

  That is the real price of provable 100-bit security on a small field:
  not a different field, not a different bound -- just many more queries.
