2. IS THE SIMPLIFIED BOUND VALID AT DEPLOYED PARAMETERS?
============================================================================================
    proof len  depth   condition t >=  t at 100 bits   holds
  ----------------------------------------------------------
         2^20     18           2^25.2          2^100     yes
         2^24     24           2^29.6          2^100     yes
         2^25     25           2^30.7          2^100     yes
         2^26     26           2^31.8          2^100     yes

  The condition binds at ~2^30 while any adversary worth bounding runs to 2^100
  or beyond, so the simplified form is the operative one everywhere. The
  length-dependent term it drops is INDEPENDENT of t -- a fixed cost of about
  2^-227 at lambda = 256 with a 2^24 proof -- so nothing of a worse shape than
  birthday survives anywhere in the additive error.

3. THE CONSTANT COSTS 0.90 CLASSICAL BITS AND 0.60 PQ BITS
============================================================================================
   lambda  classical  vs lambda/2  PQ (BHT)  vs lambda/3
  ------------------------------------------------------
      128      63.10        -0.90     42.06        -0.60
      192      95.10        -0.90     63.40        -0.60
      248     123.10        -0.90     82.06        -0.60
      256     127.10        -0.90     84.73        -0.60
      384     191.10        -0.90    127.40        -0.60
      386     192.10        -0.90    128.06        -0.60
      512     255.10        -0.90    170.06        -0.60

  A 256-bit digest delivers 127.10 classical bits, not 128. Any system
  claiming exactly 128 classical bits with a 256-bit hash is 0.9 bits short
  by the book's own constant.

4. CORRECTING ITERATION 26'S DIGEST REQUIREMENT
============================================================================================

  Iteration 26 said 128 PQ bits needs "a 384-bit digest", from lambda/3 >= 128.
  Carrying the constant:

      lambda = 384  ->  127.40 PQ bits   (misses 128 by 0.60)
      lambda = 386  ->  128.06 PQ bits   (clears it)

  So the requirement is lambda >= 386, not 384. The "13 field elements over a
  31-bit base" figure survives unchanged -- 13 * 31 = 403 >= 386 -- it was the
  round number 384 that was wrong, and only just.
